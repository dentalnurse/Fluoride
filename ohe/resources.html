2 PCAS Session Checklist

Every PCAS session must include all of the following to support GDC record-keeping standards. Tick each item off before submitting your practical portfolio for supervisor sign-off.

Patient identifier (initials or case number — never full name) and patient group
Patient age and gender
Date of session and topic number (1-6)
Referral source and relevant medical/social history (session 1) or changes since last visit (session 2)
Setting, aim and objectives for the session
Advice given, personalised to the patient
Evaluation of the session and reflection on your own practice
Any concerns identified or onward referral made
Supervisor name, GDC registration number and email
Supervisor sign-off confirming attendance and supervision

3 Quick Reference: DBOH Age-Appropriate Advice

Based on Delivering Better Oral Health (current edition). Always verify against the current edition.

Age Group Toothpaste Fluoride Level Amount / Method Key Diet & Brushing Advice
Under 3 years 1,000 ppm minimum (1,350-1,500 ppm if high risk) Smear Supervised brushing twice daily; only milk or water in a bottle/cup; avoid sugar in weaning foods
3-6 years 1,350-1,500 ppm Pea-sized Supervised brushing twice daily, spit don't rinse; limit sugary food/drink to mealtimes
7 years and over / adults 1,350-1,500 ppm (up to 5,000 ppm if prescribed for high risk) 2cm strip Brush twice daily, interdental clean once daily, spit don't rinse
Older adults 1,350-1,500 ppm as standard 2cm strip Watch for dry mouth from medication; adapted brush handles if dexterity is reduced; root caries risk
All ages - - Free sugars ≤5% of daily energy intake; water or plain milk between meals

DBOH = Delivering Better Oral Health (OHID/Public Health England, NHS England and Health Education England).

4 When to Refer: Limits of Scope at a Glance

If you identify any of the following during an oral health education session, escalate to the supervising dentist, hygienist or therapist rather than managing it yourself.

  • Dental pain, swelling or trauma requiring clinical assessment
  • A mouth ulcer unhealed after 3 weeks, or an unexplained red/white patch or lump
  • Signs of periodontal disease - persistent bleeding, loose teeth, recession, discharge
  • Suspected dental neglect or other safeguarding concern
  • A patient disclosure of abuse, neglect, or risk to themselves or others
  • A request for diagnosis or treatment beyond preventive advice
  • A patient or guardian declining oral health education - document and inform the supervising clinician
  • Signs of possible substance dependency requiring specialist support beyond brief intervention
  • Uncertainty about consent or capacity to receive/understand advice
  • Anything you are simply not confident or competent to address
Remember: Your role is to educate, motivate and support - not to diagnose or treatment-plan. When in doubt, always check with your supervising dentist, hygienist or therapist before proceeding.
